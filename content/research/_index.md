      title: Multilayer Optoelectronic Devices
      text: |
#        ![Multilayer optoelectronic devices](research-devices.jpg)

        Metal-halide perovskites are a remarkable class of semiconductors for optoelectronics, combining low-cost, scalable processing with optical bandgaps that can be tuned across the visible and near-infrared. This tunability makes them ideally suited to monolithically stacked multijunction solar cells, which can reach efficiencies beyond the fundamental limit of single-junction devices. Realising this potential, however, requires overcoming energy losses that grow with bandgap and optical losses inherent to stacked architectures, alongside the broader challenges of operational stability and scalable fabrication.
        
        Our research focuses on the fundamental energy and optical losses in stacked perovskite devices. We study how composition, morphology, and optical properties evolve during fabrication, and we design engineered nanostructures that extend the optical path without impeding charge transport. These materials and optical design principles carry across a range of platforms, including multijunction modules, solar-driven CO₂ conversion, and vertically stacked perovskite colour image sensors.

        We combine materials chemistry, device physics, and optical/electrical simulation to tackle these questions, and we welcome students and researchers interested in working at this interface.

  - block: markdown
    content:
      title: Facilities and Fabrication Cluster
      text: |
        Understanding a material or device requires making it reproducibly and measuring it precisely. We are building an integrated fabrication and characterisation cluster that brings together solution- and vacuum-based deposition, so that material classes usually processed in separate facilities can be grown and combined within a single high-quality workflow.

        The aim is high-precision control over the optical and electronic properties of functional materials and interfaces. This is paired with structural, optical, and electrical characterisation, including in-situ and operando measurements that track how films evolve during growth and device operation.

        This infrastructure is being established at SCUT and will include a glovebox fabrication suite, vacuum deposition systems, and current–voltage and stability testing. The group also draws on the shared characterisation facilities of the State Key Laboratory of Luminescent Materials and Devices.
