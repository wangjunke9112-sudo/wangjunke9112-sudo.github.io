abstract: "Electron transport layers (ETLs) placed between the electrodes and a photoactive layer can enhance the performance of organic solar cells but also impose limitations. Most ETLs are ultrathin films, and their deposition can disturb the morphology of the photoactive layers, complicate device fabrication, raise cost, and also affect device stability. To fully overcome such drawbacks, efficient organic solar cells that operate without an ETL are preferred. In this study, a new small\u2010molecule electron donor (<b>H31</b>) based on a thiophene\u2010substituted benzodithiophene core unit with trialkylsilyl side chains is designed and synthesized. Blending <b>H31</b> with the electron acceptor Y6 gives solar cells with power conversion efficiencies exceeding 13% with and without 2,9\u2010bis[3\u2010(dimethyloxidoamino)propyl]anthra[2,1,9\u2010<i>def</i>:6,5,10\u2010<i>d</i>\u2032<i>e</i>\u2032<i>f</i>\u2009\u2032]diisoquinoline\u20101,3,8,10(2H,9H)\u2010tetrone (PDINO) as the ETL. The ETL\u2010free cells deliver a superior shelf life compared to devices with an ETL. Small\u2010molecule donor\u2013acceptor blends thus provide interesting perspectives for achieving efficient, reproducible, and stable device architectures without electrode interlayers."
